The Practice

Therapy That Feels Relevant to Your Life

Therapy does not have to feel clinical, distant, or disconnected from the life you are actually living.

Sometimes you know exactly what is bothering you. Other times, things simply feel harder than they should — a relationship you are questioning, anxiety that will not settle, or the sense that the way you have always handled things is no longer working.

This is a place to have honest conversations, understand what is happening beneath the surface, and work toward change that feels practical and personal.

The Therapist Behind the Practice

Meet James Bain, LMSW, MBA

James founded Therapy for the Modern Man to create a more direct, practical, and human approach to therapy. His style is active and collaborative — the goal is not to talk in circles, but to understand what is happening, name the patterns that matter, and work toward real change.

Before becoming a therapist, James spent more than a decade in technology and executive environments. That gave him firsthand familiarity with responsibility, leadership, major career decisions, and the gap that can exist between looking capable and actually feeling settled.

Before You Begin

Questions People Usually Ask

Is Therapy for the Modern Man only for men?

No. Men’s mental health is central to the practice, but James also works with teens, LGBTQ+ and transgender clients, and other individuals who connect with his approach.

What is James’s approach to therapy?

Active, direct, practical, and collaborative. The work focuses on understanding what is happening, recognizing the patterns keeping you stuck, and finding more useful ways to respond.

Do you work with teens?

Yes. James works with teens of all genders from ages 13–20.

Do you offer in-person therapy?

Yes. The practice primarily provides in-person counseling in Austin. Limited virtual sessions may also be available for clients in Texas.

Do I need to know exactly what I want to work on?

No. Some people begin with a very specific concern. Others simply know something feels difficult, repetitive, or no longer sustainable. Either is a reasonable place to begin.

What happens when I reach out?

You can begin with a free consultation — a chance to share what brings you in, ask questions, and get a sense of whether the approach feels right.

There is no need to arrive with a diagnosis or a polished story.

How do I know if James is the right therapist for me?

Credentials matter, but fit matters too. The consultation lets you experience his communication style, ask questions about the process, and decide whether you feel comfortable taking the next step.

Start With a Conversation.

You do not need to have everything figured out before reaching out. You may know exactly what you want to change — or simply know that something has been feeling harder than it should.
